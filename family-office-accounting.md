# Family Office Accounting

For family office controllers and principals accounting for investments across entities.

## Accounting Capabilities

- Allocators (LPs): fund of funds, family offices, endowments, foundations and other institutional investors. Commitments, calls and distributions across every underlying fund, look-through to the holdings, and performance by vintage, strategy and manager.

- Holding companies, family partnerships, trusts and GRATs, SMAs and funds of one, and personal accounts, with ownership traced through every layer.

- Blocker tax accrued at the entity and carried to each investor behind it.

- Fund of funds look-through to the underlying holdings, on both ownership bases.

- Public and private equity, fixed income, loans, credit facilities on both sides, real estate and REITs, derivatives, hedge fund and private fund interests, and digital assets, in any currency.

- NAV, capital account statements, ILPA notices, performance, and Form PF and Form ADV answers from the same book.

- Saved analyses that refresh on the platform and can be pulled through the API.

**Four trusts, one holding company, six sleeves.** Band width is the value passing *through* each vehicle, so a holding company worth $157.5m in its legal books and controlling $3.58bn shows both values.

![The whole ownership structure as an org chart: four trusts, a holding company, a family partnership, six asset vehicles and a co-investment vehicle.](https://1494labs.com/assets/screens/structure-graph.60cc35a2.jpg)

**Every vehicle once, with its owners converging on it.** One vehicle can have several owners, so drawing the structure as separate trees would repeat the shared vehicles once per branch.

![A look-through consolidation showing legal books of $12.01bn against a consolidated $1.50bn, with a per-asset table giving legal owner, asset class, valuation basis, effective ownership, gross value and attributed value.](https://1494labs.com/assets/screens/look-through-consolidation.a6726248.jpg)

**$12.01bn of legal books, $1.50bn of it actually owned.**
The gap is the same dollar counted at each layer it passes through. Every
row carries the effective ownership that got it there (40% of $157.5m is $63.0m), computed from the capital accounts, so the
consolidation and the ownership diagram cannot disagree.
