Investment accounting for endowments and foundations

An endowment's investment office usually runs a multi-asset pool with a large private fund program inside it. The public side is reported by a custodian. The private side arrives one manager statement at a time, each at its own date, and has to be carried, reconciled and paced against the cash the pool will need.

Endowments and foundations · roughly 4 minutes

One book for the whole pool

Public securities, private fund interests and direct holdings sit on one multi-asset book. Positions reconcile against the custodian's file, and cash reconciles against imported bank statements. A statement line matches the book automatically only when exactly one movement fits it. Anything else is left for a person, and whatever is left over opens an exception on the same worklist as everything else.

The private program, with its dates showing

Each fund interest carries the as-of date and source of its last NAV statement. Late statements and estimate-to-final true-ups are booked as corrections, so a closed quarter can be corrected without disturbing what was already reported to the investment committee. Each position shows IRR, TVPI, DPI and RVPI.

What the pool actually holds

Look-through shows the companies underneath your fund interests, grouped by sector and geography, from holdings the managers report, as of any date you choose. Fund of funds accounting covers how look-through works and what happens when a manager doesn't report the size it needs.

Pacing the commitments

Unfunded commitments are shown by fund, and the liquidity projection sets expected calls against cash and available undrawn credit. Commitment pacing and liquidity forecasting covers the forecast.
