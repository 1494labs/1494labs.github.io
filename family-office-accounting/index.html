Family office accounting, through every layer of ownership

A family's wealth usually sits several entities deep. A trust owns part of a holding company, the holding company owns a family partnership, and the partnership holds the funds, the companies and the co-investments. The accounting question is what the family actually owns once those layers are taken out.

Family office · roughly 5 minutes

Count each asset once

In the reference household below, the legal books of every vehicle add up to $12.01bn. The family owns $1.50bn of it. The difference is the same money counted again at each layer it passes through.

Each row in the consolidation carries the effective ownership that got it there, and that ownership is computed from the capital accounts. Forty percent of a $157.5m holding company is $63.0m, and the ledger knows it is forty percent because that is what the capital accounts say. The consolidation and the ownership chart are built from the same numbers, so they can't drift apart.

How look-through consolidation works, with the full example

The structure lives in the ledger

Holding companies, family partnerships, trusts, GRATs, personal accounts and individuals are all vehicle types. Ownership weights come from capital holdings, so when a trust distributes or a sibling's interest changes, the structure chart and the allocations move with the capital.

Income, expenses and gains cascade up through the ownership chain, including where one vehicle has several owners. The allocation screen previews the cascade before you confirm it.

Trusts and their beneficiaries

Beneficiary interests are recorded against the trust that holds them. The net worth view shows each beneficiary's interest beside the trust, so a principal can see what sits in trust for each family member without a separate schedule.

Private funds and companies inside the family

Family vehicles hold LP interests in outside funds, direct company stakes and co-investments. Capital calls and distributions on those fund interests are booked against the vehicle that holds them. Fund NAV statements are recorded with an as-of date and a source, and company marks carry a valuation method, so the age of every input is on the page.

A family with a large private fund program usually needs two more things: a view through each fund to the companies underneath it, and a forecast of the cash its commitments will draw. Fund of funds accounting covers the first and commitment pacing covers the second.

What the family sees

Net worth by asset category, by holder and by period, with the movement between periods. Household performance, principal cash flows, exposure and concentration, and a reconciliation of book value to market value.

Report packs are organized by audience, so a pack for the principals and a pack for the trustees can run off the same book for the same period. Each exports to PDF or CSV.

What we don't do

We keep the books. We don't hold custody or move money: the platform produces the payment file, and someone on your team takes it to the bank. Tax returns and K-1s stay with your preparer. Market data comes from your own provider, loaded through a connection we maintain.
