For context: published starting prices elsewhere in this segment run $24,000 to $36,000 a year, before implementation. A manager running one fund currently has no option that is not a five-figure enterprise purchase. That is the gap this is aimed at.

How fund accounting software is priced

Four axes are in common use. They are not interchangeable, and which one a vendor picks tells you what they think they are selling.

AxisWho uses it, and why it suits them
Per AUM basis points The fund administrator's model. Their cost genuinely rises with the size and activity of the book, because people do the work, and basis points track that. Software does not carry that labor, so the same fee charges a growing customer more for no extra cost incurred — and punishes exactly the customer worth keeping.
Per seat Suits software whose value scales with headcount. Fund accounting does not. A two-person administrator running forty funds would pay almost nothing, which is precisely backwards.
Flat platform fee Simple, and honest about the cost of having a customer at all. Alone it ignores how much book they actually keep, and treats five funds and fifty as the same thing.
Per fund, with a platform minimum What we use. A fund is a book: its own calendar, its own terms, its own close. The per-fund component tracks the unit of work and the unit of value; the minimum covers the cost of having a customer at all, which is the cost that actually binds a small team.

The market also splits on whether it publishes at all. The self-serve end, aimed at emerging managers with one or two funds, posts a monthly price. The entity-count end quotes, and usually adds an implementation fee. By segment we sit on the quote side.

What drives a quote up

  • ✓Fund count. Each fund is a separate book with its own calendar and its own close.
  • ✓Structural complexity. A master-feeder with series accounting, equalization and side pockets carries more mechanism than a single closed-end vehicle.
  • ✓Conversion of an existing book. Migrating mid-life funds means carrying opening balances and fee-engine state — high-water marks, loss carryforwards, equalization position — rather than starting from inception.

Nothing else should move it much. Users do not, storage does not, and the size of the AUM does not.

What is included

  • Onboarding, at no separate fee. Implementation fees are normal at this end of the market and often run to five or six figures. We do not charge one.
  • Every vehicle type on the same ledger. Open-end and closed-end, registered funds, family-office structures. Not separate products with an integration story between them.
  • The morning sweep. The invariant checks that re-prove every fund's arithmetic daily, and the break worklist a failure lands in.
  • The public API. The same one every screen in the product runs on. There is no private back channel with more in it.
  • Support from the people who wrote it. A consequence of being small rather than a tier.

Why the entry price is published and the rest is quoted

Because they are different kinds of claim. What a single-fund manager pays is a decision — we are choosing it, and nothing about it waits on a measurement. What a twenty-fund conversion costs is a measurement, and we have not taken it.

Two things a larger quote has to cover are still genuinely untested: how long a real conversion takes on somebody's actual book rather than a seeded one, and how many customers one person can carry well. We have views on both. Neither is evidence yet, and a figure derived from them would be the same kind of confident unsourced number this product exists to refuse.

The axis will not change. Above one fund, a quote is a conversation about fund count, structure and whether you are converting.

Founding partners

The first cohort is capped at five, and the cap is real rather than scarcity theatre: a founding partner takes the most hand-holding while paying the least, and five is what a small team can carry without the service degrading for everyone else.

The exchange, in both directions:

  • Onboarding is free, and that is the discount. Not a percentage off the recurring rate. A permanent discount creates a cohort that can never be repriced and is also the reference customer, so the discount outlives the reason for it. Waiving a five-to-six-figure implementation fee is a real concession that does not damage the rate.
  • A founding rate, time-boxed, with the step to standard agreed at signature rather than negotiated later. The step being pre-agreed is the part that makes it survivable for both sides.
  • What you give: a nameable reference and a case study, real books to test against, design input while the product is still soft, and tolerance for defects — against a stated response expectation, so it is not open-ended in either direction.

For the right people, advisory involvement can stand in for some of the money. That is often worth more to both sides.
