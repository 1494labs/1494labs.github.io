What counts as a fund

A master and three feeders is four entities and one economic book. The feeders pick up master P&L through real internal positions; the work is not four times a standalone fund, and counting it that way would get the answer wrong. Master-feeder, parallel funds, feeders, SPVs and blockers are quoted rather than counted.

Share classes, series and sleeves within a fund are not separate funds. A fund with seventeen series is one fund.

Converting an existing book

Quoted — and free for the five founding partners. A new fund set up here is part of the subscription. Bringing across a book that already exists is different work, and it is the one thing we cannot price honestly yet, because we have not done one for a customer.

What a conversion covers: your opening position at a conversion date — balances, capital accounts, and the fee-engine state that makes the next incentive calculation correct, which means high-water marks, loss carryforwards and equalization position. Periods before that date are carried as reporting-only history and marked pre-conversion, rather than re-derived. Reconstructing history you do not have — re-deriving prior periods so they compute rather than report — is a larger project and quoted on its own.

There is still no implementation fee, for anyone. The distinction is that setting the platform up is ours to absorb; carrying ten years of somebody else's book across is work whose size we do not yet know.

How fund accounting software is priced

Four axes are in common use. They are not interchangeable, and which one a vendor picks tells you what they think they are selling.

AxisWho uses it, and why it suits them
Per AUM basis points The fund administrator's model. Their cost genuinely rises with the size and activity of the book, because people do the work, and basis points track that. Software does not carry that labor, so the same fee charges a growing customer more for no extra cost incurred — and punishes exactly the customer worth keeping.
Per seat Suits software whose value scales with headcount. Fund accounting does not. A two-person administrator running forty funds would pay almost nothing, which is precisely backwards.
Flat platform fee Simple, and honest about the cost of having a customer at all. Alone it ignores how much book they actually keep, and treats five funds and fifty as the same thing.
Per fund, with a platform minimum What we use. A fund is a book: its own calendar, its own terms, its own close. The per-fund component tracks the unit of work and the unit of value; the minimum covers the cost of having a customer at all, which is the cost that actually binds a small team.

The market also splits on whether it publishes at all. The self-serve end, aimed at emerging managers with one or two funds, posts a monthly price. The entity-count end quotes, and usually adds an implementation fee. By segment we sit on the quote side.

What drives a quote up

  • ✓Fund count, which is published above rather than quoted — each standalone fund is a separate book with its own calendar and its own close.
  • ✓Structural complexity. A master-feeder with series accounting, equalization and side pockets carries more mechanism than a single closed-end vehicle, and its entity count is not a measure of the work in either direction. This is the one that makes a quote a conversation.
  • ✓Conversion of an existing book, and separately whether you want history reconstructed rather than carried. Migrating a mid-life fund means bringing opening balances and fee-engine state across; re-deriving prior periods so they compute rather than report is a project on top of that.

Nothing else should move it much. Users do not, storage does not, and the size of the AUM does not.

What is included

  • Onboarding, at no separate fee. Implementation fees are normal at this end of the market and often run to five or six figures. We do not charge one.
  • Every vehicle type on the same ledger. Open-end and closed-end, registered funds, family-office structures. Not separate products with an integration story between them.
  • The morning sweep. The invariant checks that re-prove every fund's arithmetic daily, and the break worklist a failure lands in.
  • The public API. The same one every screen in the product runs on. There is no private back channel with more in it.
  • Support from the people who wrote it. A consequence of being small rather than a tier.

Why the entry price is published and the rest is quoted

Because they are different kinds of claim. What a manager pays for standalone funds is a decision — we are choosing it, and nothing about it waits on a measurement. What a linked structure costs, or a conversion of an existing book, is a measurement, and we have not taken it.

Two things are still genuinely untested: how long a real conversion takes on somebody's actual book rather than a seeded one, and how many customers one person can carry well. We have views on both. Neither is evidence yet, and a figure derived from them would be the same kind of confident unsourced number this product exists to refuse.

The axis will not change, and neither will the published scale. A quote is a conversation about structure, and about bringing an existing book across — not about how many funds you have, which is on this page.

Founding partners

The first cohort is capped at five, and the cap is real rather than scarcity theatre: a founding partner takes the most hand-holding while paying the least, and five is what a small team can carry without the service degrading for everyone else.

The exchange, in both directions:

  • Conversion is free, and that is the discount. Everyone gets onboarding without a fee; founding partners get their existing book brought across without one too, which is the part that is otherwise quoted. Not a percentage off the recurring rate — a permanent discount creates a cohort that can never be repriced and is also the reference customer, so it outlives the reason for it. Waiving a five-to-six-figure conversion is a real concession that leaves the rate intact.
  • A founding rate, time-boxed, with the step to standard agreed at signature rather than negotiated later. The step being pre-agreed is the part that makes it survivable for both sides.
  • What you give: a nameable reference and a case study, real books to test against, design input while the product is still soft, and tolerance for defects — against a stated response expectation, so it is not open-ended in either direction.

For the right people, advisory involvement can stand in for some of the money. That is often worth more to both sides.
