Per fund, with a platform minimum

A single fund is $500 a month. Each standalone fund after it is $200. Onboarding is included and there is no implementation fee. Everything below is how that number is arrived at, what moves it, and which parts we quote instead of publishing.

What counts as a fund

A master and three feeders is four entities and one economic book. The feeders pick up master P&L through real internal positions, so the work is not four times a standalone fund, and billing it that way would be wrong. Per entity it is also more intricate than a single fund, so the count misses in both directions. Master-feeder, parallel funds, feeders, SPVs and blockers are quoted rather than counted.

Share classes, series and sleeves inside a fund are not separate funds. A fund with seventeen series is one fund.

Converting an existing book

Setting up a fund that starts its life here is part of the subscription. Bringing across a book that already exists is different work, and it is quoted. It is also free for the five founding partners, which is what that programme is.

A conversion carries your opening position at a conversion date: balances, capital accounts, and the fee-engine state that makes the next incentive calculation correct, which means high-water marks, loss carryforwards and equalization position. Periods before that date come across as reporting-only history, marked pre-conversion rather than re-derived. Rebuilding periods you do not have, so that they compute rather than report, is a larger project and quoted on its own.

There is still no implementation fee, for anyone. Standing the platform up is ours to absorb. Carrying ten years of somebody else's book across is work whose size we do not yet know.

How fund accounting software is priced

Four axes are in common use. They are not interchangeable, and which one a vendor picks tells you what they think they are selling.

AxisWho uses it, and why it suits them
Per AUM basis points The fund administrator's model. Their cost really does rise with the size and activity of the book, because people do the work, and basis points track that. Software carries no such labor, so the same fee charges a growing customer more for no extra cost incurred, and penalizes the customer most worth keeping.
Per seat Suits software whose value scales with headcount. Fund accounting does not. A two-person administrator running forty funds would pay almost nothing, which is precisely backwards.
Flat platform fee Simple, and honest about the cost of having a customer at all. On its own it ignores how much book they keep, and treats five funds and fifty as the same thing.
Per fund, with a platform minimum What we use. A fund is a book: its own calendar, its own terms, its own close. The per-fund part tracks the work and the value, and the minimum covers the cost that actually binds a small team.

The market also splits on whether it publishes at all. The self-serve end, aimed at emerging managers with one or two funds, posts a monthly price. The entity-count end quotes, and usually adds an implementation fee. By segment we belong on the quote side, which is most of why this page exists.

Why some of this is quoted

Because published and quoted are answering different questions. What a manager pays for standalone funds is a decision. We are choosing it, and nothing about it waits on a measurement. What a linked structure costs, or a conversion of an existing book, is a measurement, and we have not taken it.

Two things are genuinely untested. How long a real conversion takes on somebody's actual book rather than a seeded one, and how many customers one person can carry well. We have views on both. Neither is evidence, and a figure derived from them would be the same kind of confident unsourced number this product exists to refuse.

So the axis will not change and neither will the scale. A quote is a conversation about structure, and about bringing an existing book across. It is not a conversation about how many funds you have, which is on this page.

Founding partners

The first cohort is capped at five. The cap is real rather than scarcity theatre: a founding partner takes the most hand-holding while paying the least, and five is what a small team can carry without the service degrading for everyone else.

Conversion of an existing book is free, which is otherwise quoted and is the larger half of the offer. The recurring rate is half the published scale for twelve months and then steps to standard, agreed at signature rather than negotiated later. One fund is $250 a month instead of $500. Five funds are $650 instead of $1,300.

Twelve months rather than twenty-four, because the cost a founding partner carries is front-loaded. Defects, design calls and being first are worst in the first half of a year, and a discount should not outlive the thing it pays for.

In exchange we ask for a nameable reference and a case study, real books rather than a pilot on sample data, design input while the product is still soft enough for it to matter, and tolerance for defects against a stated response time so the expectation is bounded on both sides. For the right people, advisory involvement can stand in for some of the money, and is often worth more to both sides.

What else we compete on

The entry price is low because nothing is currently aimed at a manager running one fund, not because cheap is the position. For software that touches money the buyer's question is not whether it will be expensive but whether it will be wrong about their books, and that is what the rest of this site is about.

We expect onboarding to take days against the six-to-eighteen-month implementations normal at this end of the market. That is an intention rather than a measurement, it is exactly what the founding cohort exists to test, and we will publish what it actually turns out to be. Waiving the implementation fee is a decision, so that one is true today. The morning sweep we have found no equivalent of in this segment. And terms in public, in a corner of the market that publishes nothing, is a claim this page is also the evidence for.
