# Notes

Working notes on the parts of fund accounting that are hard to look up and
harder to get right. Written for controllers, CFOs and administrators who have
to make the numbers defensible.

- [## Converting from a fund administrator: prove the first close

Agree the handoff date, reconcile the opening records and reproduce the next fee calculation before accepting the new book.](https://1494labs.com/notes/converting-from-a-fund-administrator/)

- [## Equalization at conversion: check each subscriber separately

Two investors can hold the same number of shares and need different fee outcomes. Test the imported records investor by investor.](https://1494labs.com/notes/equalization-at-conversion/)

- [## High-water-mark acceptance tests before the first live close

Use a recovery, a partial redemption and a gain above the mark to check that the fee engine retained the right investor state.](https://1494labs.com/notes/high-water-mark-acceptance-tests/)

- [## Side pockets: keep the investment and its investors together

A side pocket needs its own participation record. A later subscriber should not silently acquire an interest in an earlier illiquid investment.](https://1494labs.com/notes/side-pockets-and-investor-participation/)

- [## Management fee offsets: reconcile the fee before and after the credit

A fee offset is a calculation with a source, a percentage and a period. Keep all three visible, along with any amount carried forward.](https://1494labs.com/notes/management-fee-offsets/)

- [## Moving a fund off spreadsheets: prove the opening book first

Choose a cutover date, carry the calculation state and run a parallel close. A trial balance alone is not a complete fund conversion.](https://1494labs.com/notes/moving-a-fund-off-spreadsheets/)

- [## A wrong number that agrees with itself

A break gets investigated because something disagrees. When the
roll-forward foots and the statement ties, nothing raises its hand,
and the figure can still be wrong. What it takes to catch that, and
why it runs every morning.](https://1494labs.com/notes/a-wrong-number-that-agrees-with-itself/)

- [## ABOR and IBOR, and why most systems keep them apart

Two books, two clocks, and a reconciliation nobody wants to own. Where the
split came from, what it costs, and four questions that separate the
architectures faster than a feature matrix.](https://1494labs.com/notes/abor-and-ibor/)

- [## American vs European waterfall on the same two deals

The same two deals through both structures. Same fund, same total profit,
and a six million dollar difference in what the manager is holding when the
second deal disappoints.](https://1494labs.com/notes/european-vs-american-waterfall/)

- [## Equalization, and what a mid-period subscriber actually owes

An investor who buys after a drawdown rides the recovery without paying an
incentive fee, because the loss being carried forward is somebody else's.
Why one NAV per share cannot be right for two investors, and the two ways
out.](https://1494labs.com/notes/equalization-and-the-mid-period-subscriber/)

- [## High-water marks and the partial redemption

Half the money leaves. The loss carried forward against the half that
stayed has to fall by half too, and when it does not, nothing in the
accounts disagrees. The error only ever runs one way.](https://1494labs.com/notes/high-water-marks-and-partial-redemptions/)

- [## What an opening balance has to carry

A trial balance says where a fund stands, which is less than it takes to compute what happens next. A fund converted without its high-water marks reconciles
perfectly for exactly one period.](https://1494labs.com/notes/what-an-opening-balance-has-to-carry/)

- [## Which columns can be added, and which cannot

A weighted average and an average of averages differ by $300,000 a year on
a $100m fund, and both look like a subtotal. Four classes of column, three
behaviors, and why a blank cell is an answer.](https://1494labs.com/notes/which-columns-can-be-added/)
