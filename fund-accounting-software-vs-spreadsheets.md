# 1494 Labs vs spreadsheets for fund accounting

Compare a spreadsheet-based fund close with a ledger that traces fees, allocations and investor balances to their entries.

Updated November 3, 2026

## Compare the work of closing a period

A useful evaluation starts with one completed period from a fund like yours. Reproduce the fee, allocation and investor statement, then change one input and follow its effect through the book.

| Controller's task | Spreadsheet workflow to inspect | 1494 Labs demonstration to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Update a fund's fee terms | Locate the formulas and copies that depend on the term | Show the configured fee terms and the resulting calculation |
| Explain an investor balance | Trace workbook references to ledger exports | Trace the capital account to allocations and entries |
| Check the close | Review reconciliations and exception tabs | Show the checks and investigate an exception |
| Prepare the next period | Verify copied formulas, opening balances and fee state | Show the next period's opening state and calculation trail |

## Use a worked acceptance test

1. Agree the opening ledger, investor balances and fee state.

2. Run the period with the same dated transactions and terms.

3. Reconcile NAV, fees and every investor's closing balance.

4. Introduce a corrected input and inspect the affected entries and reports.

5. Save the evidence and agree who owns the accepted book.

The objective is a reproducible close. Keep independent spreadsheet checks where they help a reviewer, and make the accounting record and approval process clear.
