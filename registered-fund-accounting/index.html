The obligations that come with a registered wrapper

An interval fund, a RIC or a BDC is a normal book carrying a second set of duties. The duties run on a clock, and they are answerable to shareholders rather than to an auditor once a year.

Solutions · roughly 5 minutes

A second set of duties on the same book

Tell shareholders what a distribution was actually paid out of. Repurchase up to a fixed share of the fund on a published schedule. Stay inside a coverage ratio. Each of those is computed from the same ledger that produces the NAV, on the fund’s own tax year and its own calendar.

A notice that is sourced, not asserted

When a fund pays out more than it earned, it has to tell shareholders how much of the check was return of capital. The number is not a disclosure written alongside the payment; it is the payment, decomposed. Sourcing it from the book is what makes the year-to-date figure add up across every notice the fund has sent.

Repurchase offers on the clock

Board approval, a request deadline that freezes the queue, pricing at a named NAV version, and proration when requests exceed the offer. What happens to a residual is the part that varies: it either cancels or carries forward to the next offer as a standing claim on the ledger, whichever the fund’s own terms say. Both are configured rather than assumed.

Managed distributions

A policy in the fund’s own vocabulary: a NAV percentage or a fixed amount, at a cadence, drawing on sources in a stated order. Behind every declaration sits a declared-to-paid lifecycle, record-date shares and per-holder characterization.

Qualification and excise

The distribution requirement and the excise calendar are assessed against the book as it stands, on the fund’s own tax year, rather than reconstructed from statements at year end.

BDC controls

The asset-coverage ratio is a gate rather than a report. Alongside it: a two-part incentive fee with an income hurdle, a catch-up and a capital-gains part on its own lookback, and the ten-year senior-securities table the disclosure asks for.
